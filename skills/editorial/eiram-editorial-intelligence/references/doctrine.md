# EiRAM Analytic and Editorial Doctrine

## Analytic mission

Define the problem clearly. Anticipate developments. Provide judgments and insight. Tell the consumer what is happening, why it matters, what could change, and what action follows.

## Reasoning discipline

- Separate information from intelligence. Information is collected material. Intelligence is evaluated information organized for a decision.
- Start with the key intelligence question.
- Make assumptions explicit.
- Judge source access, credibility, corroboration, recency, and relevance separately.
- Consider at least one plausible alternative explanation.
- Use calibrated confidence: low, moderate, or high.
- Explain what would change the judgment.
- Avoid mirror imaging, confirmation bias, anchoring, availability bias, groupthink, and motivated reasoning.
- Prefer diagnostic evidence over merely consistent evidence.
- Do not confuse absence of evidence with evidence of absence.

## Writing discipline

- Lead with the judgment.
- Use focused headings and topic sentences.
- Keep paragraphs short enough to preserve argument visibility.
- Prefer active voice when the actor is known.
- Turn nominalizations into verbs.
- Remove clichés, bureaucratic padding, and false precision.
- Use qualifiers only when they change meaning.
- Keep parallel ideas in parallel form.
- Distinguish fact, assessment, forecast, and recommendation.

## Editorial integrity

- Preserve the user's intended force without preserving errors.
- Remove generic AI framing, repeated conclusions, empty transitions, artificial enthusiasm, and unsupported certainty.
- Do not imitate a living author's distinctive voice.
- Do not fabricate experience, quotations, citations, or evidence.
- End when the argument is complete.
