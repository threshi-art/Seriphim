# Legal Research Output Contract

Use this structure for a legal-research or issue-spotting response.

## Scope and limits

- State the legal question, jurisdiction, as-of date, requested depth, and procedural posture.
- Say whether sources were independently reviewed or supplied by the requester.
- State that the result is research and issue-spotting, not representation, legal advice, or a guaranteed legal conclusion.

## Issue map

For each issue, provide:

1. The question and applicable elements, standards, defenses, remedies, or procedural rule.
2. Material facts that support, undermine, or are missing from the issue. Label legal assumptions separately.
3. The authority hierarchy derived for the named jurisdiction, court or forum, as-of date, and issue. Identify binding primary authority in that forum, then contextually relevant persuasive authority and secondary explanation; do not use a universal fixed ranking. Include court or issuer, jurisdiction, date, precedential status, pinpoint when available, and a source link when available.
4. Competing interpretations, adverse authority, exceptions, negative treatment, and unresolved splits.
5. Procedural uncertainty: forum, burden, deadlines, preservation, record limits, review standard, or other unknowns.
6. A bounded assessment using conditional language tied to the stated facts and authorities.

## Currency, gaps, and next steps

- Identify the last verified date and any authority, statute, rule, or docket that still needs currency checking.
- List fact and record gaps, including information that could change the analysis.
- Identify research steps needed to confirm controlling or adverse authority.
- Recommend qualified counsel when the stakes, deadline, or uncertainty makes individualized professional advice necessary.
