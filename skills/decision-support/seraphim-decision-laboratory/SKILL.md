# Seraphim Decision Laboratory

## Overview

Build an auditable decision record that distinguishes what is known from what is assumed. Improve the decision owner's understanding and options; do not make a consequential choice on their behalf.

## Intake and boundaries

Collect the decision owner, decision statement, options, constraints, evidence, assumptions, uncertainties, and time horizon. Ask focused questions for material gaps; state any working assumption instead of silently filling it.

Include a genuine **status-quo** option: maintain the present course, defer, or take no new action. Do not label it neutral; identify its costs, risks, and irreversible effects.

Minimize sensitive inputs. Do not request credentials, private identifiers, or confidential details unless essential; summarize them at the least identifying level. Treat decision support as analysis, not legal, medical, financial, or safety-critical authority. Escalate to a qualified professional where the decision requires it.

## Run the comparison

1. Separate facts (source, date, confidence) from assumptions (owner, rationale, test) and uncertainties (unknown, likelihood, impact). Score each uncertainty as low, medium, or high and explain the score.
2. Define decision criteria from constraints and objectives. Compare every option, including status quo, against the same criteria. Do not conceal a criterion that changes the ranking.
3. Mark each option's commitment as reversible, partially reversible, or difficult to reverse. Identify the cost, time, and trigger for reversal.
4. Identify sensitivities: the assumption, estimate, or criterion weight whose plausible change would alter the comparison. Name the evidence or experiment most likely to resolve each material sensitivity.
5. Seek disconfirming evidence for the leading option and plausible alternatives. Do not present absence of evidence as evidence of absence.
6. State a recommendation only as conditional decision support: why it fits the current record, what would change it, and what remains the owner's choice.

## Produce the decision record

Read and use [the decision-record contract](references/decision-record-contract.md). Keep entries compact enough to inspect and update. End with checkpoints: owner, next evidence action, review date or decision trigger, and conditions that reopen the decision.

## Quality checks

Before presenting the record, verify that:

- every option uses the same comparison criteria;
- facts, assumptions, and uncertainties are visibly distinct;
- the status-quo option is substantive;
- uncertainty, reversibility, sensitivities, and disconfirming evidence are explicit; and
- the final consequential choice remains with the decision owner.

## Common mistakes

- **False precision:** do not turn weak estimates into exact-looking scores; explain judgment and uncertainty.
- **Option theater:** do not compare favored options with weaker criteria or omit status quo.
- **Recommendation as instruction:** do not imply authority, pressure the owner, or hide value judgments.
- **Unowned follow-through:** do not finish without a named owner and a checkpoint.
